# Message delivery semantics

Status: proposed production-readiness contract and risk inventory. The retry, inbox/outbox, idempotency, and replay standards below are not implemented as a shared platform today.

## Scope and current platform behavior

This specification covers every application `IConsumer<T>` in `src` at commit `15365b8` and the order saga because it owns the highest-risk delivery workflow. Endpoint ownership is cross-checked against `messaging-topology.json`.

All contracts inherit `BaseMessage` and therefore carry `EventId`, `CorrelationId`, `TriggeredAt`, `CreatedBy`, `Source`, and `Version`. `EventId` is the transport-delivery deduplication key. A producer retry that constructs a new message also constructs a new `EventId`, so high-impact operations additionally require the business key shown below.

The current shared registration in `Extensions.MassTransit.cs`, `MessagingHostConfigurator.cs`, `RabbitMqTransportConfigurator.cs`, and `AzureServiceBusTransportConfigurator.cs` configures no retry, delayed redelivery, inbox, or outbox. An unhandled consumer exception therefore follows the transport's MassTransit error behavior after the first attempt (an endpoint error queue on RabbitMQ; the configured Azure Service Bus error/dead-letter strategy must be made explicit). A consumer that catches or suppresses an exception is acknowledged and never reaches an error queue.

## Consumer inventory

“I/O” means inbox/outbox. “Required” means correctness depends on both deduplicating receipt and atomically recording outgoing messages with the owned state whenever a transactional store exists. “Inbox” means deduplication is needed but there is no state-plus-publish transaction. Payload sensitivity is High for personal, financial, credential-like, or precise fulfillment data; Moderate for internal identifiers or inventory; Low for public catalog data.

## Saga delivery boundary

`OrderStateMachine` in `src/Orchestration/Orchestration/Saga/OrderStateMachine.cs` correlates all workflow events by `OrderId`, persists state in PostgreSQL with optimistic concurrency, schedules payment timeout, and emits payment, stock, basket, shipping, operations, and order commands. It has no EF outbox. A state commit and its sends/publishes are therefore not an atomic unit, and optimistic-concurrency retries are not configured. The saga must use the EF transactional outbox and deterministic effect identifiers such as `OrderId + source EventId + effect name`; consumers must retain the same keys during replay.

## Standard delivery policy

### Error classification and retries

- Permanent: schema/validation failure, unsupported enum/state, invariant violation, authentication/authorization failure, malformed URI/identifier, or provider 4xx other than 408/409/423/429. Do not retry. Record a sanitized reason and move to error/dead-letter handling.
- Ordering-not-ready: referenced aggregate not yet visible or an expected predecessor not processed. Use delayed redelivery only when the contract explicitly allows eventual arrival; exhaust to error, never acknowledge silently.
- Transient: timeout, connection reset, broker/database unavailability, optimistic concurrency, HTTP 408/429/5xx, or provider-declared retryable response. Preserve cancellation and retry budget.
- Immediate retry: at most 2 retries with jittered delays near 100 ms and 500 ms, only for short transient failures. Never immediate-retry external money movement or non-idempotent provider POSTs unless an idempotency key is accepted by the provider.
- Delayed redelivery: 3 attempts at approximately 30 seconds, 2 minutes, and 10 minutes with jitter. Provider-specific `Retry-After` takes precedence. The total attempt budget is shared across immediate retry and redelivery headers and must be observable.

### Inbox, outbox, and idempotency

- Persist inbox receipt (`consumer`, `EventId`, received/processed timestamps, outcome) with a unique constraint before acknowledging. Retain at least the maximum broker redelivery/replay window.
- Use a transactional outbox for state changes plus sends/publishes. EF services use the MassTransit EF outbox in the same `DbContext`; MongoDB services need a transactionally written outbox collection where supported. Redis-only consumers require a durable inbox or atomic Lua/transaction key guard in a durable store.
- An in-memory outbox may reduce duplicate sends during a single attempt but is not the production correctness boundary.
- External providers receive the documented business key in their idempotency header/request. Persist provider request and response before publishing success. A timeout after submission is “unknown outcome” and must reconcile by key, not blindly resubmit.
- Duplicate receipt returns the stored prior outcome without repeating state mutation, publish, email, billing, refund, stock increment, or provider call.

### Correlation and trace propagation

- Set MassTransit `CorrelationId` to the contract `CorrelationId` (`OrderId` for order workflows and `CustomerId` for customer events), preserve `ConversationId` and `InitiatorId`, and keep W3C trace context across send, publish, schedule, retry, and redelivery.
- `MessageId` identifies an envelope; `EventId` identifies the logical message; neither replaces the business operation key when a producer can reconstruct a message.
- Logs and traces may include order/payment/reservation/shipment IDs under the data policy, but never email, postal address, tax ID, document URL, signature, token, full provider payload, or message body.

### Error, dead-letter, and replay

- Standardize one error destination per endpoint and a separate skipped/unroutable destination. Preserve original body, contract type, endpoint, `EventId`, correlation/trace headers, attempt counts, exception type, and sanitized failure category.
- Emit `Fault<T>` only to an explicitly owned fault consumer; do not rely on it as the sole alert. Alert on error arrivals, age, retry exhaustion, and backlog.
- Quarantine poison/security-invalid messages; do not loop them through delayed queues. Replay is an authenticated, audited operation that reuses the original `EventId` and business key.
- Consumers must not swallow correctness failures. A deliberately ignored permanent event must produce a structured terminal outcome metric/log; missing aggregates classified as ordering races must redeliver.

### Graceful shutdown

- On shutdown, stop accepting new deliveries, allow in-flight handlers to finish within a 60-second host budget, propagate the host token, and stop the bus after handlers/outbox flush. Do not acknowledge until state and inbox/outbox are durable.
- Provider and database calls must use bounded timeouts shorter than the shutdown budget. Cancellation is not a fault/dead-letter condition when initiated by host shutdown; the broker should redeliver.
- Verify both transports with a test that terminates a worker mid-handler and proves exactly one durable effect after restart.

## Ownership split

Shared platform work: configure retry/redelivery/error conventions, transport-parity tests, correlation/trace filters, host shutdown budget, reusable inbox/outbox registration, and delivery metrics. Bounded-context work: choose business keys, make mutations conditional, add domain lifecycle fields/unique indexes, classify domain errors, integrate provider idempotency/reconciliation, and write crash/replay tests for each consumer. P0 order is stock release/commit, refund/payment, order mutation/saga, shipping provider calls, then seller finance and notifications.
